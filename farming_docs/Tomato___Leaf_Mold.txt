CROP: Tomato
DISEASE: Leaf Mold
PATHOGEN/CAUSE: Passalora fulva (fungus, syn. Fulvia fulva / Cladosporium fulvum)

SYMPTOMS:
Pale green to yellow spots on upper leaf surface; olive-green to grayish-brown velvety mold growth on the underside directly beneath the spots. Affected leaves curl, wither, and drop. Mainly a greenhouse/high-humidity problem.

FAVORABLE CONDITIONS FOR SPREAD:
High humidity (above 85%) and moderate temperatures, common in greenhouses or densely planted, poorly ventilated fields.

ORGANIC / NON-CHEMICAL TREATMENT:
Improve ventilation and reduce humidity around plants. Remove and destroy infected leaves. Copper-based fungicide sprays as a preventive measure.

CHEMICAL TREATMENT:
Fungicides such as chlorothalonil or mancozeb, especially in greenhouse settings where humidity is hard to control. Check local recommendations for approved products.
(Note: Always confirm exact product names and dosages with a local agricultural extension officer or product label, as recommendations vary by region and formulation.)

PREVENTION:
Increase plant spacing and prune lower leaves for airflow. Use fans/ventilation in greenhouses. Avoid overhead watering and water early in the day so foliage dries quickly. Choose resistant tomato varieties where available.
