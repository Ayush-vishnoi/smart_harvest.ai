CROP: Apple
DISEASE: Apple Scab
PATHOGEN/CAUSE: Venturia inaequalis (fungus)

SYMPTOMS:
Olive-green to brown velvety spots on leaves and fruit, which later become darker and scab-like with a corky texture. Severely affected leaves may yellow and drop; fruit can become cracked and distorted.

FAVORABLE CONDITIONS FOR SPREAD:
Cool, wet spring weather during bud break and early leaf growth. Spores overwinter in fallen leaves and are released during rain in spring.

ORGANIC / NON-CHEMICAL TREATMENT:
Rake and destroy fallen leaves in autumn to reduce overwintering spores. Sulfur-based or copper-based fungicide sprays applied from bud break through early summer.

CHEMICAL TREATMENT:
Fungicides containing myclobutanil or captan, applied on a protective schedule from green tip stage through early summer, especially during wet periods. Confirm timing and rates with local orchard extension guidance.
(Note: Always confirm exact product names and dosages with a local agricultural extension officer or product label, as recommendations vary by region and formulation.)

PREVENTION:
Choose scab-resistant apple varieties for new plantings. Prune trees for good air circulation and sun penetration. Clean up fallen leaves each autumn. Avoid overhead irrigation that prolongs leaf wetness.
