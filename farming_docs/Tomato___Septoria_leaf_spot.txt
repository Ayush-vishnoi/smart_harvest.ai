CROP: Tomato
DISEASE: Septoria Leaf Spot
PATHOGEN/CAUSE: Septoria lycopersici (fungus)

SYMPTOMS:
Numerous small, circular spots with dark brown margins and gray/tan centers, often with tiny black dots (fungal fruiting bodies) visible in the center. Starts on older, lower leaves and progresses upward. Heavy infection causes significant leaf drop.

FAVORABLE CONDITIONS FOR SPREAD:
Warm, wet, humid weather. Spreads via rain splash, overhead irrigation, and contaminated tools/debris.

ORGANIC / NON-CHEMICAL TREATMENT:
Remove infected lower leaves as soon as spotted. Copper-based fungicide sprays applied preventively. Mulch soil to reduce spore splash from ground debris.

CHEMICAL TREATMENT:
Fungicides with chlorothalonil or mancozeb, applied on a 7-10 day schedule during favorable wet conditions. Confirm exact rates with local guidelines.
(Note: Always confirm exact product names and dosages with a local agricultural extension officer or product label, as recommendations vary by region and formulation.)

PREVENTION:
Rotate crops away from tomato/solanaceous family for 1-2 years. Avoid overhead watering. Stake plants and prune for airflow. Clean up and destroy plant debris at season end, since the fungus survives on old leaf litter.
